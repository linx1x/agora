Agora 2.5D styles

The same Jungle corner (the waterfall, the pond, a desk pod on each side) in three styles, by day and in the evening, with the new paper avatars. Every image was made in code from Agora's own art. At the end, pick the engine, the style, the tilt and the avatar look; your picks are saved for Claude.

Pixel diorama

What it isToday's tiles, unchanged, on 3D shapes: desks are extruded from their art, walls get height, plants and chairs stand up. A tilt-shift blur and soft glow give the toy-model (Octopath "HD-2D") look.
Art costLowest. Every wing already has its tiles, so all of HQ can be converted without redrawing.
Watch forThe avatars (48 × 64) are sharper than the 32 px tiles. Pixels can shimmer while the camera moves; you can check that in the benchmark's "Walk around".

Storybook paper

What it isThe same tiles redrawn automatically: smoothed to 4×, ground painted into flat patches, a white cut-out edge on every object, a paper grain over everything. Closest to Paper Mario in spirit.
Art costHighest. This is only a direction: a real version means drawing every tile again in this style, wing by wing.
Watch forThe automatic redraw leaves rough pond and path edges and keeps some pixel detail on desks. A hand-made pass would fix both.

Low-poly models

What it isSimple flat-shaded models built in code (desks with legs, chairs, cone plants, block trees), with no textures. Real shadows read best here.
Art costMedium. Each object type needs a small model, but no texture work.
Watch forIt loses the character of the current art, and the paper people look less at home among solid models.

Camera tilt

Pixel diorama by day at four tilts. At 45° and steeper, the upright paper people get squashed because the camera looks down on them. 30–35° keeps them readable.

Paper avatars

Built from today's avatar choices (skin, hair, colours, glasses, headphones), so saved avatars carry over. Four directions; the last frames of each row are the card flip when turning left ↔ right.
